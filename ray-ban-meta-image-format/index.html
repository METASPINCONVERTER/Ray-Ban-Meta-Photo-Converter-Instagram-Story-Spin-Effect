Ray-Ban Meta Image Format: 3024×4032 and 3:4 Ratio Explained

Why Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses use a vertical 3:4 sensor geometry and how pixel dimensions determine Instagram feature detection.

By rayBEN Technical Team • 6 min read • Updated March 2026

Hardware Sensor Specifications

The Ray-Ban Meta Smart Glasses (Gen 2) integrate an ultra-compact 12-Megapixel camera module embedded into the right temple frame. Unlike conventional smartphones where portrait photos are typically shot in 4:3 (e.g. 3024 × 4032 rotated) or full vertical 9:16 (1080 × 1920), the physical sensor on the glasses is mounted vertically:

  • Native Pixel Matrix: 3024 horizontal pixels by 4032 vertical pixels.
  • Megapixel Count: 12,192,768 pixels (~12.19 MP).
  • Aspect Ratio: Exactly 3:4 portrait (0.75 ratio).
  • Field of View: ~122° ultra-wide lens with software rectilinear rectification.

Why 3:4 Instead of 9:16 (Full Screen Story)?

Instagram Stories display vertically in 9:16 (0.5625 aspect ratio). However, smart glasses shoot in 3:4 (0.75) for two vital technical reasons:

  1. Parallax Margin (Motion Padding): When Instagram renders the 3D parallax spin effect, it needs extra image area along the outer boundaries so that foreground objects can shift without exposing empty black edges as the user rotates or tilts their phone.
  2. Optical Quality: A 3:4 sensor utilizes the center circle of the miniature glass lens with minimal corner chromatic aberration and vignetting.
The Geometry Check in Instagram

Instagram's Story engine checks the physical pixel dimensions. If an image is 3024 × 4032, Instagram recognizes the glasses sensor profile. If the image is cropped to 9:16 (e.g., 2268 × 4032) or standard 4:3 landscape (4032 × 3024), Instagram disables the 3D spin view badge.
